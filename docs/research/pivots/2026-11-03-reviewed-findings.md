**Early confirmation of temporary price pivots: engineering research for Trading**

Researched November 3, 2026. This is an indicator design and benchmark proposal. No detector has been implemented or evaluated on the project's data, and no detection latency or accuracy is established for GC or SI. The recommendation combines independently inspected primary sources, official documentation, and project code. The [Parallel report](2026-11-03-parallel-report.md) is supporting research; this document records the engineering assessment. Start the next task with the [agent handoff](../../project/pivot-detection-handoff.md).

Follow-up to the PR #66 review: the [pivot/structure contract](../../project/pivot-structure-design.md) now defines confirmed HH/HL/LH/LL, provisional HL/LH, a trade-path detector published per second and per minute, and the initial six-scale storage grid. It refines this research proposal's implementation sequence: validate deterministic correctness and store that grid before benchmarking predictive usefulness. Swing magnitude comes from the threshold; neither the threshold nor sampling resolution guarantees duration. The research below remains a record of evaluated method families, not a benchmark result.

The strongest starting design is a small family of causal directional-change detectors, accompanied by one local-trend reversal detector and, if it adds measurable value, a calibrated classifier. They answer different questions: what reversal has occurred, whether the recent directional tendency has changed, and whether a provisional extreme is likely to satisfy a specified future outcome. Keep these outputs separate initially. Their agreement is evidence to evaluate, not three independent votes.

**The target must specify scale and duration.**

A price can be a low over ten seconds, remain in a larger downtrend, and be exceeded a minute later. There is no unique pivot without a definition. Specify the incoming move, minimum outgoing reversal, permitted break of the extreme, observation horizon, sampling stream, and plateau policy. “Temporary” requires a horizon or an event endpoint.

At an apparent extreme, two possible continuations can share exactly the observed history and then behave differently. Therefore, no method using that history can certify the unrestricted future. This argument does not rule out probabilistic forecasting, nor exact recognition of a finite, already satisfied rule. Computation time and the delay needed to acquire evidence are different constraints; unlimited computation does not provide later observations.

The directional-change literature explicitly distinguishes the extremum from its subsequent threshold confirmation. Tsang, Ma, and Chinthalapati also investigate nowcasts made before that confirmation. Their FX experiments support studying early recognition, not assuming that a particular accuracy or latency transfers to these futures. Their reported coverage varies substantially across currency pairs. [Directional-change nowcasting, 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/isaf.1552).

Keep three times in every experiment: the observed extreme's time, the detector's estimate of the turning time if different, and the time the output became available. A later estimate of an earlier turn is useful, but the indicator first becomes available at the later time.

**Recommended detector 1: directional change at several scales.**

The original financial event-time construction tracks a running high or low until an opposite price move crosses a declared threshold, then changes direction. It naturally separates the endpoint of the preceding leg from the later observed crossing. [Glattfelder, Dupuis, and Olsen, algorithm and pseudocode](https://arxiv.org/html/0809.1040v2).

My proposed contract for this project is as follows. Work internally with a dimensionless log-relative price, or raw prices confined to detector data context, and publish only normalized features. In an upward leg retain high H and its ordinal e. A high is threshold-confirmed when an available observation p satisfies:

    10_000 * log(H / p) >= reversal_threshold_bps

For a low L, use 10_000 \* log(p / L). Change direction only on a satisfied reversal condition; initialize explicitly before the first complete leg. The emitted event contains e and the current availability time. A later break of H or L becomes a subsequent outcome or new event, not an edit to what was observed earlier.

Compare fixed-bps thresholds with volatility-normalized thresholds. For the latter, snapshot a trailing scale from information available no later than the candidate extreme, and freeze it for that candidate. Replace the candidate and snapshot only under a declared new-extreme rule. A declining volatility estimate must not, by itself, turn an unchanged price into a newly confirmed reversal.

Test a deliberately small scale grid. Values such as 0.25, 0.5, and 1.0 times the chosen trailing ATR are illustrative experiment settings, not validated parameters. Include a normalized spread/noise floor so the experiment does not primarily identify bid/ask bounce. Maintain separate states for separate scales; do not assume their events form a perfectly nested tree, especially with adaptive thresholds.

Outputs should include reversal magnitude, incoming-leg magnitude and duration, age of the candidate extreme, scale identifier, and event status. The first crossing is the earliest possible confirmation of this particular crossing rule. It is not proof of statistical optimality under another pivot definition. A sampled price can also jump through the threshold, so the realized displacement can be larger than its nominal value.

**Recommended detector 2: evidence that local direction reversed.**

The most direct alternative is to estimate local slope and uncertainty using only observations available now. A local-linear state-space model has a latent level and slope; a turning signal requires evidence that a previously negative slope is now positive, or the reverse. Fit noise and adaptation parameters on training history. Publish a normalized slope or slope-to-uncertainty statistic, and distinguish the estimated trend turn from the raw observed extremum. Statsmodels documents the local-linear model and separate filtering and smoothing interfaces. [Local-linear model](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/examples/notebooks/generated/statespace_local_linear_trend.html), [Kalman filtering](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.tsa.statespace.kalman_filter.KalmanFilter.filter.html).

Bramante, Facchinetti, and Zappa propose online peak/trough identification through tests on consecutive rolling-regression slopes, with simulations and S&P 500 index applications. The publisher abstract establishes that method family; access here did not expose the full paper, so its exact test, repeated-testing behavior, and intraday applicability remain unverified. It is a useful challenger, not evidence of GC/SI performance. [Online detection of financial time series peaks and troughs, 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sam.11411).

An alternative within this role is a directional sequential likelihood test on normalized, disjoint returns or model residuals. FOCuS evaluates change-in-mean evidence over multiple possible change sizes/windows. Its research supports that statistical construction, rather than a claim that it discovers financial pivots. [FOCuS paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v24/21-1230.html).

Require an actual sign reversal: a drift changing from strongly positive to weakly positive is deceleration; a variance change is neither a high nor a low. Repeated monitoring, autocorrelation, heavy tails, and heteroskedasticity make textbook significance thresholds unreliable without empirical validation. The quickest-detection literature establishes optimality under specified statistical models and false-alarm criteria, rather than universally for financial pivots. [Quickest change detection](https://arxiv.org/html/1210.5552). Calibrate the complete alarm/reset policy on representative training sessions. Compare a slope method against one CUSUM/FOCuS variant; do not add every variant before determining whether this family improves the benchmark.

The author-maintained Python package changepoint-online exposes Focus and distinguishes stopping_time from its estimated changepoint; that is a useful reference implementation for research. Runtime compatibility and suitability for this engine have not been tested. [Python package documentation](https://pypi.org/project/changepoint-online/).

There is a further reason to avoid interpreting these as independent votes. Algebraically, a zero-reference one-sided cumulative sum of returns is a drawup from the running cumulative minimum. Some simple CUSUM configurations therefore closely resemble a directional-change rule. Additional complexity must demonstrate incremental information.

BOCPD is a reasonable later comparison: its original formulation infers the posterior distribution of the current run length. A pivot application additionally needs signed regime means/slopes and evidence of opposite directions; a run-length reset alone is insufficient. Use posterior summaries appropriate to the actual model, rather than assuming a generic “change probability” measures reversal confidence. If outlier sensitivity becomes a measured failure, a robust Bayesian variant is a research option; its general robustness results do not establish futures-pivot performance. [Robust BOCPD research](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/altamirano23a.html). [Adams and MacKay](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742).

**Optional detector 3: probability that a provisional extreme will qualify.**

Start with logistic regression and compare one gradient-boosted tree model, such as LightGBM. This is an interpretable experimental baseline and a nonlinear challenger; it is not a sourced claim that either is the best financial pivot classifier. [LightGBM's model documentation](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/).

Emit candidates causally, including candidates that later fail. A probability should answer a complete question, for example: conditional on the current information, will price move a specified distance away from this observed low before breaking it by an invalidation buffer, within the next K scheduled-open rows? Record the reference level, frozen scale, barriers, horizon, episode identity, and availability time at candidate creation. Do not quietly replace the level later and retain the original prediction's timestamp.

For a low with frozen scale s, a diagnostic outcome can use L + R*s as the reversal barrier and L - C*s as invalidation; mirror for highs. R, C, and K are part of the target. If the reversal condition has already happened when scoring, it is an observed confirmation rather than a prediction of that condition. Score earlier candidates or explicitly choose a different prospective target. Do not give the model credit for forecasting an already crossed barrier.

Separate completion from survival: a candidate can complete its required reversal and later break the old extreme. Completion remains a valid historical event; survival through a declared deadline is a different target. Neither should be silently substituted for the other. A strict point target and a target allowing a small overshoot (a pivot zone) also need different names.

Separate reversal, invalidation, fully observed timeout, and unknown outcome caused by unavailable follow-up. A known timeout can be its own class or a non-event under the declared finite-horizon task; it must not disappear from reporting. Survival/competing-risk models become useful if multiple horizons or genuine observation censoring justify their added complexity.

Future-confirmed outcomes are legitimate supervised labels. Leakage occurs when future information enters the available input, candidate selection, preprocessing, or validation split. Training only on hindsight-selected successful extremes would conceal the hard failed candidates.

Calibrate on a later, disjoint chronological subset of training history and validate the actual first-alarm policy, not just all candidate snapshots. Use reliability diagrams as well as Brier score and log loss: the latter measure more than calibration alone. [Scikit-learn calibration guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html).

Sokolovsky and Arnaboldi provide related ES price-level reversal/crossing research, but established-level reactions are not identical to recognizing a fresh extreme. Their revised evaluation reports weak/uncertain effects for the tested pattern. It supports careful component evaluation, not a transferable accuracy promise or the strongest claims from earlier versions. [Revised primary study](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.09993v4).

**Volume is an input hypothesis, not a confirmation rule.**

Useful ablations include aggressor delta divided by known-sided volume, signed-volume persistence, relative trade intensity, relative volume, normalized price response per relative volume, failed attempts to extend an extreme, and sampled L1 imbalance with observation age. Heavy selling without a new low can be an exhaustion/absorption proxy; it does not prove that passive absorption caused a future reversal. Unknown sides require explicit coverage information.

Cont, Kukanov, and Stoikov find that full best-quote order-flow imbalance explains short-interval price changes more robustly than trade volume in their equity sample. That imbalance includes limit orders and cancellations. It is not the same as trade delta. Gould and Bonart find next-mid-price-direction information in queue imbalance for Nasdaq stocks; that is a one-tick task with different observations and instruments. [Order-book event study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402), [Queue-imbalance study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03492).

Databento TBBO supplies trades and the BBO immediately before each trade, in trade space. It does not observe intervening quote events. Thus sampled L1 features are usable, but continuous quote changes, complete OFI, cancellations, and queue replenishment cannot be reconstructed from this archive. [Official TBBO schema](https://databento.com/docs/schemas/tbbo).

**Other approaches remain useful with explicit availability.**

Fixed left/right extrema provide a simple finite-window reference. A right span of k requires k subsequent rows of the chosen stream before output. A two-row right span on a per-phase minute-scale stream is not two seconds. Declare plateau equality handling, require meaningful movement, and do not treat stale flat prices as fresh evidence. [Official Pivots documentation](https://python.stockindicators.dev/indicators/Pivots/).

SciPy's local maxima, prominence, width, and distance filters are retrospective path descriptions. The basic local maximum needs a right neighbor; richer selection can depend on later observations. ZigZag libraries may redraw the last segment. Prefix-only recomputation can be causal if each output is frozen and first availability is recorded, but plotting the final full-history result does not reproduce that stream. [SciPy peak detection](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.find_peaks.html), [ZigZag repaint warning](https://python.stockindicators.dev/indicators/ZigZag/).

Likewise, ruptures is an offline segmentation library. It can support a retrospective reference or a carefully specified prefix-recomputation experiment; its standard batch output is not an online pivot feed. Centered smoothers, full-history HMM decoding, and future-conditioned state smoothing need the same timing discipline. A fixed-lag smoother may estimate an older turn causally at the current time, provided the delay is retained. [Ruptures documentation](https://centre-borelli.github.io/ruptures-docs/).

Topology/persistent extrema and scale-space methods can help describe which swings survive changes in scale, but the financial topology paper inspected here concerns path decomposition/scaling, not demonstrated early turning-point recognition. [Primary topology paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08860).

TEASER explicitly separates sequence classification from deciding whether enough of the sequence has arrived. This is relevant to choosing when to emit an early estimate. Its benchmark success is not a futures-pivot result; a financial experiment needs causal episode starts and unfinished/failed episodes. It is a later comparison once tabular baselines exist. [Original TEASER paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-020-00690-z), [Aeon implementation](https://www.aeon-toolkit.org/en/stable/examples/classification/early_classification.html).

I found no inspected primary evidence establishing that a sequence/foundation model is more reliable than the proposed simpler methods on this project's inputs and horizon. More computation is permission to test a challenger, not evidence that a larger model will identify the turn earlier.

**Fit to the current codebase.**

The follow-up replays the engine's released trades for every scale, publishing fast state per second and slow state per minute, with rolling features supplying context. The follow-up contract defines one swing history per contract/stream/scale and a deterministic market-structure layer. Per-phase rolling-candle fractals can remain explicitly named benchmarks; they are not the strategy's chronological swing structure. Conventional candle indicators still retain their per-phase histories. Do not combine 60 near-duplicate outputs as independent confirmations.

Use slot_ordinal for membership, phases, ages in open rows, and episode horizons. Preserve elapsed wall time separately when useful; closures do not create observations. Compute each contract independently, initialize/reset under a declared gap/session policy, and never detect a price jump across a delivery-month switch as a pivot. A missing row is not a flat market. A covered no-trade row advances the scheduled grid but contains no new trade/quote price; models need a declared stale-observation policy.

Begin with close-based price observations so ordering is unambiguous. Current BaseBars also preserve high_at and low_at, which help localize observed extremes; those fields do not encode every threshold crossing or all repeated intrasecond excursions. If a result depends on path order, inspect/replay DBN trades using the same release/availability rules. An event's finer observed extreme time does not move its feature availability earlier than the second close and configured delay.

State belongs in strategy-core's shared causal pipeline, with normalized FeatureSpec/Unit outputs and versioned checkpoints. Raw reference prices are event/data context. Outputs may include side, scale, candidate/confirmed status, age, reversal magnitude, incoming-leg magnitude, directional evidence statistic, calibrated probability with target/horizon identifier, and validity/observation age. A downstream strategy should be free to use any subset.

Inspection of the current repository proposal identified four corrections: its blanket statement that future pivot labels leak is too strong; iter_candidate_outcomes produces simulated fill-anchored trade outcomes rather than extreme-anchored pivot outcomes; a fully observed timeout is different from missing follow-up; and profitability is not the selection gate for the indicator requested here. The research implemented no detector, labeling pipeline, model, or database change. These findings and the supporting reports are now archived as documentation.

**The first benchmark should settle whether complexity helps.**

Predeclare a small set of independent reference tasks spanning temporary swing scales and horizons. One reference can use finite left/right neighborhoods plus minimum incoming/outgoing excursions; another can use a fixed first-passage outcome. These are operational definitions, not universal truth. Generating labels independently of any detector's emitted candidates lets the benchmark measure missed episodes and candidate coverage.

Replay directional-change rules, fixed-right-span extrema, a local-slope method, and one directional CUSUM/FOCuS challenger on the same history. Then compare logistic regression with one GBDT on a common candidate set. Run price-only, price-plus-volume, and price-plus-volume-plus-valid-sampled-book ablations. Compare variants at matched recall or false-alarm burden, rather than declaring the most selective detector best because it emits very few signals.

Measure event precision/recall with predeclared one-to-one matching, false alarms per scheduled-open hour, missed events, duplicate alarms, extreme-location error, median and tail availability delay in open rows and wall time, and normalized displacement from the extreme. Score pre-extreme alarms separately from post-extreme recognition. Include full event coverage and the fraction of the target move consumed by confirmation. Add reliability diagrams and proper probability scores for models.

Use chronological calendar blocks across products, keep related candidates within an episode together, purge overlapping outcome intervals, and use an explicit boundary gap/embargo justified by the target and dependence. Fit preprocessing, scales, calibration, and thresholds within training periods. Reserve the newest holdout, report GC/SI and session/liquidity strata separately, and estimate uncertainty by resampling sessions or larger calendar blocks rather than pretending every second is independent. Keep the parameter search budget small.

Finally, verify prefix invariance, full-session versus one-second parity, checkpoint continuation, price/size scale invariance, and behavior around plateaus, closures, missing coverage, late trades, and rolls. These tests establish causal implementation correctness; only held-out market experiments establish detection quality. The decision criterion is the measured delay/error/coverage trade-off. Profitability can be investigated later by strategies that consume the indicator.
